Sources & checks

Each case follows one public publication and connects its claim to a specific study and a formal correction or retraction. Separate publications using the same research have separate case pages linked to each other. Source links, document pages and publication dates are provided on the evidence page.

Cases were researched and checked independently by separate agents, then reviewed together. Each evidence page gives the source check date, publication date and date of the research change.

Major and minor changes

Each case is marked Major or Minor. This is an editorial assessment of how much the research change matters to the claim the source makes.

A case is major when the cited finding was retracted or withdrawn, when the corrected figure changes the conclusion the source draws (it reverses, crosses a threshold the source relies on, or differs by roughly a fifth or more), or when the use is high-stakes, such as clinical guidance, legislation or public finance, and the change affects what a reader would do.

A case is minor when the figure moves only slightly, stays within its original uncertainty or leaves the conclusion unchanged, and the use is low-stakes.

Starting points

The search used Retraction Watch, its public dataset via Crossref, publisher notices and the public pages that reuse the findings. German-speaking countries, the UK and recent changes were prioritised.
