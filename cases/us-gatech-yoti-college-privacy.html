A university college page retains a facial-image-sharing allegation removed from the main research release

Georgia Tech · US technology university in Atlanta · Ivan Allen College of Liberal Arts

Why it matters

The retained allegation concerns distribution of facial images collected during age checks. It is a specific, serious privacy accusation used to challenge age-verification laws. The same account explicitly connects the research to US state mandates, pending social-media restrictions and court privacy assumptions. The correction materially changes what readers are told was demonstrated about facial-image sharing.

Still says

19 May 2026 · currently available college article
a single verification attempt may transmit a user’s facial image, IP address, and device fingerprint to credit card companies.

Body · paragraph beginning According to the researchers · Open the source ↗

The college’s account of research on Yoti says an age-verification attempt may send credit-card companies a facial image, IP address and device fingerprint. It uses these claimed privacy risks to question existing and proposed age-verification laws.

What changed

CorrectedBy 19 June 2026 · exact correction date unavailable

Georgia Tech corrected its research-news article. It removed statements it says were posted in error and not part of the researchers’ findings or intent. The revised account separates IP, operating-system and browser metadata from ID information. It does not say Yoti sends facial images to outside companies.

Limits and context

This is a formal correction to the university’s research-news article. It is not a retraction or established correction of the IEEE paper. The revised release still reports privacy concerns about metadata and some ID information. It does not establish that all age verification is private or safe. Yoti’s own account that facial images never leave its systems is an interested-party claim. It is used here to identify the disputed statement and report its dated response. The university correction has no separate date. 19 June dates Yoti’s acknowledgment and contemporaneous reporting, not a verified modification time. The correction concerns facial-age-estimation image sharing with outside third parties. It must not be applied to all identity verification. The paper’s Table 3 separately describes ID/manual face-photo processing involving Yoti India. A broader outside-companies claim in independent commentary was therefore excluded as insufficiently exact.

How the claim connects to the study

The college page and corrected News Center page share the exact title, original 19 May date, contact John Popham, research team, and author-hosted AgeVerification.pdf link. The college page retains the specific facial-image-to-outside-company statement removed from the corrected main account, whose formal notice says earlier statements were not the researchers’ findings or intent.
