A climate-finance explainer still uses the retracted $38 trillion estimate

Council on Foreign Relations·23 October 2024

Retracted3 Dec 2025

Still says

around $38 trillion

How much money is needed and how much has been invested? · Open the source ↗

The backgrounder says climate damages will cost around $38 trillion per year by 2050, linking to the study’s institutional press release.

What changed

Nature retracted the underlying economic-damages paper after data and analysis problems required changes too substantial for a correction.

The original study estimated income losses against a no-climate-impact baseline, in 2005 international dollars. This case concerns continued use of that withdrawn estimate; the retraction does not imply zero climate damages.

Why it matters

The estimate appears in the section explaining how much climate finance is needed, giving readers a scale for major funding decisions.

How the claim connects to the study

CFR’s amount links to the Potsdam Institute’s original release, which identifies Kotz, Levermann and Wenz’s Nature paper. That release now carries an update about the retraction; the CFR paragraph does not.
