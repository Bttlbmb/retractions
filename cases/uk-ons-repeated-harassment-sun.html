MajorUnited Kingdom·Crime & justice

A crime-policy article retains an ONS undercount of repeated-harassment victims

The Sun · UK daily newspaper · Jordan Farrell

Why it matters

The retained count is approximately 600,000 below the corrected rounded estimate. The underlying official trend also reverses direction. The article places the figures in a debate about stalking protection, prosecution and specialist training.

Still says

2 August 2026 · article remains publicly available
They added that an estimated 1.1million people experienced repeated harassment in the last year.

Paragraph immediately before the Crime and Policing Act discussion · Open the source ↗

The Sun attributes an estimated 1.1 million repeated-harassment victims in the year ending March 2026 to the Office for National Statistics (ONS), alongside reporting on stalking protection and the government's violence-against-women policy.

What changed

Corrected13 Aug 2026

On 13 August 2026 the ONS corrected a derivation error in repeated-harassment estimates. The March 2026 estimate rose from 2.2% (around 1.1 million people) to 3.5% (around 1.7 million). The March 2025 comparator fell from 3.1% to 2.3%. The reported annual decrease became an increase.

Limits and context

The Sun repeats the obsolete count, not the erroneous decrease. Its headline and main police-recorded stalking/harassment figures are separate measures, not invalidated by this correction. Repeated harassment covers people aged 16 or over in England and Wales, not the whole UK. It is a survey prevalence estimate, not a count of police-recorded incidents. The approximate 600,000 difference uses rounded published counts. No deliberate concealment is established.

How the claim connects to the study

The Sun explicitly attributes the surrounding harassment estimates to the ONS and repeats the exact 1.1 million count and year from section 10 of the original March 2026 the Crime Survey for England and Wales (CSEW) bulletin. Its article does not link directly to that bulletin. The ONS current bulletin links its superseded version, allowing the old and corrected estimates and trend to be checked directly.
