A council revenue guide still cites a retracted experiment for its deadline advice

Local Government Association · Behavioural Insights Team·15 March 2018 · guide still supplied

Retracted2 Sept 2026

Still says

Behavioural research suggests that a deadline can help overcome procrastination and prioritise task completion

Page 8 · Use timely prompts and reminders · footnote 18 · Open the source ↗

The LGA and Behavioural Insights Team guide says deadlines can help overcome procrastination and prioritise task completion. Its reference for that claim is the retracted Ariely–Wertenbroch experiment.

What changed

Psychological Science retracted the experiment in September 2026 after concerns about the underlying datasets and a replication and data investigation undermined confidence in its findings. Both authors agreed.

The deadline claim cites the withdrawn experiment. The guide’s adjacent text-message payment trials and planning-prompt findings cite separate sources. The retraction does not determine the effectiveness of every intervention in the guide.

Why it matters

The guide offers practical recommendations to councils collecting council tax, fines and other revenue.

How the claim connects to the study

Footnote 18 follows the procrastination and task-completion claim on page 8. The bibliography on page 12 identifies the exact Ariely and Wertenbroch title, year, journal and pages. The LGA publication page continues to link the joint guide.
