Germany·Social policy

German planning guidance repeats the original gender care gap

Federal Ministry for Housing, Urban Development and Building·May 2025 · second edition

Corrected28 Mar 2024

Still says

Durchschnittlich verwenden sie täglich 43,8 Prozent mehr Zeit für unbezahlte Sorgearbeit als Männer.

Printed page 4 · case for gender-equitable planning · Open the source ↗

Federal guidelines for gender-equitable urban planning say women spent 43.8% more time on unpaid care than men, a difference of 77 minutes a day. The same figures remain in a Bundestag caucus explainer.

What changed

Destatis corrected a data-processing error in March 2024. The estimate rose to 44.3%, or 79 minutes a day. A separate June 2025 revision using census-based weights put it at 43.4%, or 76 minutes.

All three estimates are for 2022 and compare average time spent on unpaid work by women and men aged 18 or older. The March 2024 correction and the June 2025 census reweighting are separate events.

Why it matters

The guidelines use the figure to explain why urban planning should consider people doing unpaid care work. The Greens’ parliamentary group also uses the survey to discuss care and employment policy.

How the claim connects to the study

The Greens’ caucus directly links the original Destatis release. The planning guidance cites a Verkehrsclub Deutschland article, which gives the original figures and points to the family ministry’s information on the Destatis survey.
