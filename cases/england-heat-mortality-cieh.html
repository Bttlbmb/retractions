United Kingdom·Climate & environment

Environmental health reporting keeps the original England heat-mortality estimate

Chartered Institute of Environmental Health · Environmental Health News·5 October 2023

Corrected24 Jul 2026

Still says

an estimated 51,670 deaths in England, and 2,186 deaths in Wales, were associated with the hottest days

ONS results for 1988–2022 · Open the source ↗

Environmental Health News reports the original ONS estimate of 51,670 deaths associated with England’s hottest days from 1988 to 2022.

What changed

The Office for National Statistics identified 51,670 as an incorrect estimate in July 2026. Its methods guide says an error in the analysis code substantially overestimated deaths associated with the hottest days at England level.

The ONS notice names the England total of 51,670. The August 2026 replacement reports 40,063 for a longer period, 1988–2025, with updated methods and a narrower definition of the hottest days.

Why it matters

The professional reporting presents the result to environmental health practitioners alongside expert comments about climate-related mortality and public health.

How the claim connects to the study

The article names the ONS report, repeats its original England estimate and quotes the ONS statistician. The formal notice identifies the same 51,670 total.
