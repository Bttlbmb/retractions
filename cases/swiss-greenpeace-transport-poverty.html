MajorSwitzerland·Climate & environment

A Swiss transport-policy appeal retains the Europe-wide access claim Greenpeace corrected

Greenpeace Switzerland

Why it matters

The unsupported continental-majority claim accompanies a concrete Swiss campaign for flight-ticket and private-jet levies to fund transport benefits. The corrected international average is about 15%, while 56% is Cyprus’s national maximum.

Still says

19 May 2026
Über 50 Prozent der Menschen in Europa haben keinen Zugang zu öffentlichen Verkehrsmitteln.

Opening paragraph and following explanation · Open the source ↗

In EnglishGreenpeace Switzerland says over 50% of people in Europe have no access to public transport and presents the finding alongside support for the Mobilitätsbon popular initiative.

What changed

CorrectedCorrection date unclear (2026)

Greenpeace Germany formally corrected its press interpretation: the 56% figure concerns Cyprus, while the average across the countries studied is about 15%. The correction concerns Greenpeace’s summary of Access Denied, rather than a withdrawal or correction of the underlying Öko-Institut report.

The underlying report remains published. Its availability indicator includes absent public transport, low frequency or inconvenient schedules; it is not a literal count of people with no service anywhere nearby. The country average is population-weighted and this indicator uses 2024 EU-SILC data. Over 50% not using public transport regularly is a separate finding and was not withdrawn. The original press-release date is 19 May 2026, but the date when its correction was added is not established.

How the claim connects to the study

The Swiss release directly links Access Denied: Transport Poverty in Europe at greenpeace.at, the same report linked by Greenpeace Germany’s corrected release. It calls the Europe-wide over 50% claim a central study finding. The German correction explicitly corrects that geographic extrapolation; figure 5-3 supports the country-specific interpretation.
