MajorUnited Kingdom·Social policy

JRF’s public benefits-reform campaign retains an eightfold child-poverty impact estimate

Joseph Rowntree Foundation · UK poverty and social-policy charity · public LinkedIn campaign

Why it matters

The child-poverty impact used to advocate a benefits reform is eight times the corrected estimate; the total-person estimate is about 4.3 times the corrected value. The Joseph Rowntree Foundation (JRF) says its policy narrative and conclusions would not change, but the magnitude of the claimed immediate benefit changes substantially.

Still says

2025/26 campaign · exact post date unavailable
a minimum floor would lift 130,000 additional people out of poverty in 2025/26, including 80,000 children.

Main post · Applied to all recipients paragraph · Open the source ↗

JRF’s public campaign says a protected minimum floor in Universal Credit would lift 130,000 people, including 80,000 children, out of poverty in 2025/26, and urges the government to introduce it.

What changed

Corrected2026 · exact correction date unclear

JRF disclosed a coding error in its original October 2025 briefing. For the same 2025/26 scenario, the projected number lifted out of poverty should have been 30,000 people, including 10,000 children, rather than 130,000 and 80,000.

Limits and context

This compares original and corrected coding for the same 2025/26 policy scenario. It must not be confused with JRF’s new 2027/28 estimates, made after the two-child limit was abolished. It must also not be confused with JRF’s separate very-deep-poverty estimates. The briefing defines poverty as income after housing costs below 60% of the median. It defines very deep poverty as below 40%. JRF warns of relatively low sample sizes and says it is updating later modelling. The correction page has an 11 September 2026 publication date and a further update after 22 September. The coding correction’s exact date is unclear.

How the claim connects to the study

JRF’s own post gives the same 2025/26 scenario and counts named in Note 6 of the correction. Its Read in full comment links the exact original briefing URL, which now redirects to the replacement containing that correction.
