Geneva’s medical faculty still presents a withdrawn lung cancer trial as clinical evidence

University of Geneva · Faculty of Medicine

Why it matters

The article suggests a change in treatment scheduling could improve existing care. The university was a research partner and publishes the same claim in French and English.

Still says

3 February 2026
suggèrent que le fait de programmer le traitement tôt dans la journée pourrait constituer un moyen simple et peu coûteux d'améliorer l’efficacité

Faculty of Medicine · introduction · Open the source ↗

In EnglishThe faculty’s public article presents a trial as evidence that earlier immunochemotherapy could slow the progression of advanced lung cancer. It reports 11.3 months versus 5.7 months of progression-free survival.

What changed

Retracted24 Jun 2026

Nature Medicine retracted the trial in June 2026. The editors cited discrepancies in trial registration, protocol and reporting, alongside concerns about randomisation and imaging schedules. They withdrew confidence in its results and conclusions.

Limits and context

The figures are the original trial’s median progression-free survival estimates for its two groups. The retraction applies to this trial. The faculty’s article also discusses separate research on circadian rhythms.

How the claim connects to the study

The faculty article names Huang and colleagues, links the exact Nature Medicine article and repeats its findings. The journal’s notice identifies the same study and withdraws it.
