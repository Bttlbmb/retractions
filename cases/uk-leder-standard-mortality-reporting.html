The Standard retains a corrected count and share of avoidable deaths

The Standard · London newspaper and news website

Why it matters

The figures support national reporting on NHS care failures, health inequalities and accountability for the deaths of people with learning disabilities.

Still says

2 September 2025
Some 733 (38.8%) of the 1,887 deaths of adults with a learning disability and a known cause of death

Opening findings · paragraph beginning Some 733 · Open the source ↗

The Standard reports that 733 of 1,887 reviewed deaths with known causes in 2023 were avoidable, or 38.8%. It also retains the original ranking and percentages of the leading avoidable causes.

What changed

Corrected27 Jan 2026

After withdrawing the report in December 2025, researchers replaced it in January 2026. Correcting the processing of cause-of-death data raised the avoidable share to 40.2% and changed the leading-cause ranking: cerebrovascular disease replaced digestive cancers among the top three.

Limits and context

The broad inequality finding and 19.5-year age-at-death gap remain. The January replacement gives 40.2%; the July 2026 report updates 2023 again to 41.4%.

How the claim connects to the study

The article identifies the Learning from Lives and Deaths programme (LeDeR) 2023 report and research team. Its 733 of 1,887 and 38.8% match the original table 1.12, and its causes match table 1.13. The current university project page still links the article.
