Deutschlandfunk retains a Europe-wide transport-access claim corrected by Greenpeace

Deutschlandfunk · German public national radio station

Why it matters

A country maximum is presented as a continental majority, turning the corrected approximate 15% average into over 50%. The news uses that scale in reporting proposals for public-transport subsidies and expansion.

Still says

20 May 2026 webpage · broadcast 19 May 2026
In Europa ist laut einer Untersuchung im Auftrag von Greenpeace gut die Hälfte der Bevölkerung vom Öffentlichen Nahverkehr abgeschnitten.

Headline and lead · Open the source ↗

In EnglishDeutschlandfunk says more than half of Europe’s population is cut off from public transport, attributing this to the Greenpeace-commissioned study, and reports calls for a cheaper Deutschlandticket (Germany’s nationwide public-transport ticket) and network expansion.

What changed

CorrectedCorrection date unclear (2026)

Greenpeace Germany formally corrected its press interpretation. The 56% figure concerns Cyprus; the average across the countries studied is about 15%. The correction concerns Greenpeace’s summary of Access Denied, not a withdrawal or correction of the underlying Öko-Institut report.

Limits and context

The underlying report remains published. Its availability indicator includes absent public transport, low frequency or inconvenient schedules. It is not a literal count of people with no service anywhere nearby. The country average is population-weighted and uses 2024 data from the European Union Statistics on Income and Living Conditions (EU-SILC). That over 50% do not use public transport regularly is a separate finding and was not withdrawn. The press release is dated 19 May 2026; the date its correction was added is not established.

How the claim connects to the study

The short article identifies Greenpeace’s Zugang verweigert study by the Öko-Institut and repeats its 19%, 48%, 11% contextual figures. Greenpeace Germany’s explicit correction names the same report and the precise Europe-wide half-population assertion. The study’s English title is Access Denied. This establishes the study connection without an outgoing study link on DLF.
