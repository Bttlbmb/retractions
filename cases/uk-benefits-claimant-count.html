United Kingdom·Social policy

Benefits guidance retains an IFS claimant count corrected from 900,000 to 600,000

The Independent·19 March 2025

CorrectedCorrection date not stated

Still says

around 900,000 PIP claimants worse off by £2,400 per year in 2028/29

IFS-attributed impact paragraph · Open the source ↗

The Independent’s guide to support available amid proposed benefit cuts retains an IFS-attributed estimate of 900,000 people and a conditional £2,400 annual loss. It also mislabels the affected group as PIP claimants.

What changed

IFS corrected the count from 900,000 to 600,000. Its actual population is people receiving incapacity benefits without disability benefits: UC-health claimants without PIP. The conditional £2,400 figure was not corrected.

The article additionally misidentifies the claimant group; 600,000 must not be described as a corrected PIP count. These are estimates under March 2025 proposals and assumptions about new claims, not current entitlement calculations or evidence that all proposals became law. The IFS correction has no separately stated date.

Why it matters

A national newspaper presents the outdated estimate in practical benefits guidance, shaping readers’ understanding of proposed welfare reforms and who could be affected.

How the claim connects to the study

The article explicitly attributes the count and £2,400 estimate to IFS. Its neighbouring 2.4 million families/£280 estimate also matches the same IFS response. There is no direct IFS hyperlink in that paragraph; attribution and the distinctive combination of figures establish the match.
