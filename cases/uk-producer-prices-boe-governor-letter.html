The governor’s inflation letter retains the original producer-price peak

Bank of England · Andrew Bailey·22 June 2023

Corrected22 Oct 2025

Still says

from peaks of 24.4% and 19.6% in mid-2022.

Page 2 · producer and consumer price inflation · Open the source ↗

The Bank of England governor’s letter to the Chancellor gives 19.6% as the mid-2022 peak in annual UK producer output price inflation.

What changed

The Office for National Statistics corrected an error in how it linked successive years of price data and updated the historical index weights. The final corrected July 2022 rate was 18.0%.

The 19.6% is the output-price peak. The paired 24.4% is a separate input-price estimate. The letter gives several considerations behind the interest-rate decision. ONS corrected the output peak through both a chain-linking fix and changes to historical weights.

Why it matters

The letter uses the series to explain the relationship between producer and consumer prices in its account of inflation and the Monetary Policy Committee’s response.

How the claim connects to the study

The letter identifies input and output PPI and cites ONS and Bank calculations. The original output peak matches the official series, and the ONS correction explicitly compares it with the revised 18.0%.
