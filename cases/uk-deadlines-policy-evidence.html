United Kingdom·Public policy

A UK regulator’s evidence review still uses a deadline experiment retracted in September 2026

Competition and Markets Authority · Local Government Association·5 April 2022 · current government reference document

Retracted2 Sept 2026

Still says

Self-commitment devices can also support people to … improve productivity (Ariely and Wertenbroch, 2002; Marotta and Acquisti, 2017).

Commitment · Effect on consumers and businesses · paragraph 8.54, printed page 149 / PDF page 151 · Open the source ↗

The CMA’s review cites the Ariely–Wertenbroch experiment as support for self-commitment devices improving productivity. A council revenue guide also cites it for deadlines overcoming procrastination and helping task completion.

What changed

Psychological Science retracted the experiment in September 2026 after concerns about the underlying datasets and a replication and data investigation undermined confidence in its findings. Both authors agreed.

CMA also cites an unretracted study for productivity and warns that commitment devices can backfire. Its review is a reference document, not formal business guidance. The retraction does not invalidate all commitment methods or the separate text-message payment results in the council guide.

Why it matters

The regulator’s reference document explains online design and possible remedies for competition authorities and practitioners. The council guide applies behavioural research to tax and other revenue collection.

How the claim connects to the study

The CMA’s productivity clause names Ariely and Wertenbroch (2002); its bibliography gives the exact title, journal, volume and pages of the retracted paper. The LGA guide’s deadline claim carries footnote 18, which identifies the same paper. Both documents remain linked from current publication pages.
