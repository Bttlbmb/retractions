A UK regulator’s evidence review still uses a deadline experiment retracted in September 2026

Competition and Markets Authority

Why it matters

The regulator’s reference document explains online design and possible remedies for competition authorities and practitioners.

Still says

5 April 2022 · current government reference document
Self-commitment devices can also support people to … improve productivity (Ariely and Wertenbroch, 2002; Marotta and Acquisti, 2017).

Commitment · Effect on consumers and businesses · paragraph 8.54, printed page 149 / PDF page 151 · Open the source ↗

The CMA’s review cites the Ariely–Wertenbroch experiment as support for self-commitment devices improving productivity.

What changed

Retracted2 Sept 2026

Psychological Science retracted the experiment in September 2026 after concerns about the underlying datasets and a replication and data investigation undermined confidence in its findings. Both authors agreed.

CMA also cites an unretracted study for productivity and warns that commitment devices can backfire. Its review is a reference document. The retraction applies to the Ariely–Wertenbroch experiment and leaves the other cited studies separate.

How the claim connects to the study

The CMA’s productivity clause names Ariely and Wertenbroch (2002). Its bibliography gives the exact title, journal, volume and pages of the retracted paper. The HTML and PDF remain publicly available through the government publication record.
