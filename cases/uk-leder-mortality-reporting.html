MajorUnited Kingdom·Health

An ITV investigation retains causes of death from a withdrawn NHS-commissioned report

ITV News·2 September 2025

Corrected27 Jan 2026

Still says

LeDeR found the most common causes of avoidable deaths were flu and pneumonia (14.9%)

National findings · paragraph listing leading avoidable causes · Open the source ↗

ITV retains the original ranking and percentages for the leading avoidable causes of death in 2023: flu and pneumonia 14.9%, digestive-organ cancers 9.7%, and coronary heart disease 9.5%.

What changed

After withdrawing the report in December 2025, researchers replaced it in January 2026. Correcting the processing of cause-of-death data raised the avoidable share to 40.2% and changed the leading-cause ranking: cerebrovascular disease replaced digestive cancers among the top three.

The broad inequality finding and 19.5-year age-at-death gap remain. The January replacement gives 40.2%; the July 2026 report updates 2023 again to 41.4%.

Why it matters

The figures support national reporting on NHS care failures, health inequalities and accountability for the deaths of people with learning disabilities.

How the claim connects to the study

ITV names LeDeR, the 2023 data and the university research team. Its distinctive leading-cause percentages match the original report’s table 1.13. The current King’s College London project page still links the news report.
