A policing argument retains the inference its cited study’s authors withdrew

Manhattan Institute·3 June 2020

Retracted10 Jul 2020

Still says

no significant evidence of antiblack disparity in the likelihood of being fatally shot by police

Paragraph citing the August 2019 PNAS study · Open the source ↗

A commentary quotes a 2019 study as finding no significant anti-Black disparity in the likelihood of being fatally shot by police.

What changed

The authors retracted the paper and said its data could not support conclusions about racial disparities in the probability of being shot.

This is a dated June 2020 commentary that predates the retraction and remains publicly available. The issue is the specific probability inference, not a finding that this paper proves the opposite conclusion.

Why it matters

The study is used to argue against claims of systemic police bias in a Wall Street Journal commentary republished by a policy institute.

How the claim connects to the study

The commentary directly links Johnson et al.’s PNAS paper and quotes the exact likelihood inference addressed in the authors’ retraction statement.
