MajorUnited Kingdom·Public policy

A conservation policy toolkit still recommends a signing technique backed by retracted research

Behavioural Insights Team · UK behavioural-science consultancy · Rare

Why it matters

The guide translates research into practical interventions for conservation and sustainability organisations, including regulatory compliance and reporting.

Still says

2019 report · currently offered for download
signing at the beginning of an audit or self-assessment increases honesty.

Page 31 · Tackling dishonesty, corruption, and selfish behavior · Open the source ↗

The practitioner toolkit recommends signing at the start of an audit or self-assessment to increase honest reporting. Its supporting reference is the retracted Shu et al. paper.

What changed

Retracted13 Sept 2021

PNAS retracted the paper in September 2021 after evidence called the validity of its data into question.

Limits and context

The 2019 guide predates the retraction and remains offered through its publication page. The retraction concerns the cited signing study; it does not withdraw the toolkit’s other strategies.

How the claim connects to the study

Reference 107 on page 78 gives the authors, title, journal, volume and pages of Shu et al.’s 2012 paper. These match the original identified in the PNAS notice. The current UK office’s publication page directly supplies the same PDF.

Shu, Mazar, Gino, Ariely & Bazerman · 2012 · PNAS
Signing at the beginning makes ethics salient and decreases dishonest self-reports in comparison to signing at the end
10.1073/pnas.1209746109 ↗
