A Justice Department summary retains an inflated human-trafficking prosecution increase

US Department of Justice · Office of Justice Programs

Why it matters

The statutory report informs public understanding of the criminal-justice response to human trafficking. The retained summary makes the decade increase about four times the corrected percentage and overstates the 2023 count by 569 people, about 47%.

Still says

January 2026 · OJP catalogue labels 1 January; BJS release was 26 January
increased by 73% from 2013 to 2023 (from 1,030 to 1,782).

Highlights · second bullet · Open the source ↗

The OJP public summary says federal human-trafficking prosecutions rose 73% between 2013 and 2023, from 1,030 to 1,782 persons.

What changed

Corrected23 Jul 2026

BJS corrected a coding error on 23 July 2026: prosecution-file records with missing human-trafficking flags had been wrongly included. The revised report gives 1,213 prosecutions for 2023 and an 18% increase from 2013, rather than 1,782 and 73%.

Limits and context

This is a federal prosecution count under BJS’s offense and lead-charge definitions. It is not trafficking prevalence or an estimate of all traffickers. The revised count still rises over the decade. BJS’s own 2025 page now shows 18%; the old wording remains on the separate OJP summary. The notice covers the 2022–2024 editions. No claim of resulting legislative decisions or deliberate concealment is established.

How the claim connects to the study

The OJP summary identifies Human Trafficking Data Collection Activities, 2025, NCJ 310661, and its Full Report link opens the BJS PDF with that same title and number. Page 3 and appendix table 1 contain the corrected series, and the BJS correction log names this exact edition and coding error.
