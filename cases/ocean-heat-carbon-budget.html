ModerateInternational·Climate & environment

A climate article retains a strong policy inference after uncertainty quadrupled

Scientific American

Why it matters

The result is used to explain how quickly countries need to reduce emissions to meet major climate targets.

Still says

1 November 2018
the maximum amount of emissions humans could still emit without overshooting the Paris targets would need to be reduced by about a quarter

Opening explanation of implications for the Paris Agreement · Open the source ↗

The article says a new ocean-heat estimate suggests the remaining emissions budget for the Paris targets needs to shrink by about a quarter.

What changed

Retracted25 Sept 2019

Reanalysis increased uncertainty roughly fourfold and substantially weakened the paper’s inferences about higher ocean warming and climate sensitivity. The paper was retracted.

The central estimate changed little and the method remained valid. The notice later linked a revised paper. The missing update is the much larger uncertainty and weaker inference, rather than a reversal of evidence that oceans are warming.

How the claim connects to the study

The article links the original Resplandy et al. Nature paper, reporting its ocean-heat estimate and carbon-budget implication. The retraction explicitly weakens the inferences derived from that estimate.
