A parent treatment guide still relies on a retracted vitamin D trial

Autism Parenting Magazine · online magazine for parents of autistic children

Why it matters

Parents encounter the result while comparing treatment and supplement options. Another article on the same website already acknowledges the retraction.

Still says

Displays 7 May 2026
Vitamin D3 is also linked to improved signs and symptoms related to ASD (Saad, et al., 2018).

How to cure autism naturally · Open the source ↗

The guide cites Saad et al. and describes improved autism symptoms after four months of supplementation, with no comparable improvement on placebo.

What changed

Retracted6 May 2019

The journal withdrew the trial after missing raw data, discrepancies in reanalysis and recording irregularities undermined confidence in its findings.

Limits and context

The affected evidence is this specific trial. The finding does not assess vitamin D’s established nutritional roles or other research on supplementation.

How the claim connects to the study

The treatment guide names Saad et al. (2018) and references the trial’s PubMed record, PMID 27868194. Its link to Amber Tovey’s summary now leads to a page that explicitly reports the retraction.
