UK budget commentary uses the old £12.1 billion capital gains tax estimate

The World Financial Review · online finance and business publication · UK budget commentary

Why it matters

The article uses the old total as the denominator for residential property’s share of tax liabilities in a budget policy argument.

Still says

8 November 2026
£65.9 billion in gains and £12.1 billion in liability

Article body · residential property’s share · Open the source ↗

A guest article puts UK capital gains tax liabilities at £12.1 billion on £65.9 billion in gains for 2023/24. It uses the figures to discuss residential property’s share of the tax. It uses them to argue that the budget debate should pay more attention to business owners.

What changed

Corrected27 Aug 2026

HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) says a processing error left about 20,000 taxpayers out of its 2025 release. Its August 2026 publication fixes this and adds usual updates to returns. For that year it reports £12.773 billion in liabilities and £69.861 billion in gains.

Limits and context

The figures measure gains and liabilities for the 2023/24 tax year. Cash receipts are a separate measure. The revision includes updates to provisional figures as well as the correction.
