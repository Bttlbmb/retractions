A federal men’s health portal retains the original 50% increase in depression absence

Federal Institute of Public Health · men’s health portal·24 March 2025

Corrected31 Mar 2025

Still says

Depressionen haben im vergangenen Jahr 50 Prozent mehr Fehltage verursacht als 2023.

Opening paragraph of the republished DAK release · Open the source ↗

The federal men’s health portal republishes DAK’s claim that depression-related sick-leave days rose by 50% in 2024, from 122 to 183 per 100 insured employees.

What changed

DAK withdrew the comparison because the two years used different rules to assign diagnoses. Applying the same rule to both years gives 182.2 days in 2023 and 182.6 days in 2024, a rise of 0.2%.

The report analyses absences among 2.42 million DAK-insured employees. The corrected 2024 figure still rounds to 183 days per 100 employees.

Why it matters

The federal portal presents the old comparison in public information about mental health at work and the need for support.

How the claim connects to the study

The portal explicitly identifies its text as DAK’s 24 March 2025 press release. The formal DAK correction and April report replace the inconsistent comparison with comparable diagnosis-assignment methods.
