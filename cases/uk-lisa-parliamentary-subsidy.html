MajorUnited Kingdom·Public finance

A parliamentary call for savings reform still cites a withdrawn HMRC survey

House of Commons · Treasury Committee

Why it matters

The result supports a parliamentary argument for reform of a tax-supported savings scheme, alongside a forecast £3 billion cost over five years.

Still says

11 September 2025 · statement still online
87% … could have done so without their LISA.

Chair comment · second quoted paragraph · Open the source ↗

The committee chair cites a survey finding that 87% of respondents who used a Lifetime ISA to buy a first home said they could have bought without it, then questions whether the subsidy represents good use of taxpayers’ money.

What changed

Withdrawn26 Mar 2026

HMRC withdrew the quantitative survey in March 2026 because methodological problems meant its findings could not reliably be generalised to the wider LISA population.

This is an older statement still published without a withdrawal note. The withdrawal does not establish a corrected percentage or show that the respondents’ answers were false. The £3 billion forecast and the separate qualitative and non-holder studies were not withdrawn. A later ministerial letter to the committee acknowledges the withdrawal.

How the claim connects to the study

The statement identifies recently published HMRC research on a sample of LISA holders, one week after the quantitative holder survey appeared. HMRC identifies the IFF Research survey of 1,613 ever-holders. Contemporaneous reporting reproduces its exact 87% result; HMRC’s current record and the minister’s letter identify the withdrawn survey.
