A clinic’s weight-loss advice retains a retracted vinegar trial result

The Body Clinic · German clinic website·Published 15 October 2025 · updated 8 July 2026

Retracted23 Sept 2025

Still says

verloren die Teilnehmenden durchschnittlich 6–8 kg – ohne Änderung der Ernährung oder Bewegung.

Hilft Apfelessig wirklich beim Abnehmen? · Open the source ↗

The clinic article reports average weight loss of 6–8 kg over twelve weeks with daily apple cider vinegar. It gives dosing advice and promotes medically supervised weight-loss programmes.

What changed

BMJ withdrew the trial after results could not be replicated and analytical errors and unreliable data undermined the findings.

The article acknowledges the trial’s small sample and calls for further research. Its displayed publication and update dates follow the retraction. The retained result concerns liquid vinegar and twelve-week weight loss.

Why it matters

The article addresses people considering weight-loss treatment and uses the trial result in advice about vinegar alongside clinic care.

How the claim connects to the study

The article names the Holy Spirit University of Kaslik, the 120 participants, the four groups, the doses and the twelve-week duration. Its reference names Abou-Khalil et al. (2024). These details identify the retracted BMJ trial. The full article contains no retraction notice.
