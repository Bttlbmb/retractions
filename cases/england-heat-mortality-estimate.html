United Kingdom·Climate & environment

Climate-health commentary retains an estimate of 51,670 heat-related deaths

London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine·22 September 2023

Corrected24 Jul 2026

Still says

An estimated 51,670 deaths in England, and 2,186 deaths in Wales, were associated with the hottest days

Expert comment · summary of the ONS release · Open the source ↗

The university’s expert commentary reports an estimated 51,670 deaths associated with England’s hottest days from 1988 to 2022. The figure also remains in reporting by the Chartered Institute of Environmental Health.

What changed

The Office for National Statistics identified 51,670 as an incorrect estimate in July 2026. Its methods guide says an error in the analysis code substantially overestimated deaths associated with the hottest days at England level.

The ONS notice names the England total of 51,670. The August 2026 replacement reports 40,063 for a longer period, 1988–2025, with updated methods and a narrower definition of the hottest days.

Why it matters

The original release and the researcher’s public commentary use the results to explain the need for climate and public-health measures. The university also cites the number as an example of its research impact.

How the claim connects to the study

The university’s article links the exact ONS release and identifies an LSHTM researcher who worked on the report. The notice on the original bulletin singles out the same England total.
