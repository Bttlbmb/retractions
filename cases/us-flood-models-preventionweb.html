A disaster-risk knowledge page still presents validation claims from a retracted US flood-model comparison

PreventionWeb · UNDRR disaster-risk knowledge platform

Why it matters

The retained summary presents a model comparison as validation relevant to flood-risk pricing. Choosing or rejecting models on that basis could affect insurance, adaptation and risk-reduction decisions. The retraction removes the basis for treating the paper’s model-ranking and overestimation claims as validated conclusions, rather than supplying a routine numerical update.

Still says

2026 · page last checked 24 February 2026
First Street significantly overestimates flood losses nationally and Karen Clark & Company substantially underestimates them in Florida

Second summary paragraph · Open the source ↗

PreventionWeb’s summary says catastrophe models used for National Flood Insurance Program pricing closely match historical losses within about 4%. It says First Street significantly overestimates national flood losses and Karen Clark & Company substantially underestimates Florida losses.

What changed

Retracted16 September 2026

The authors retracted the article. They found that the Table 5 data for the Florida Public Flood Loss Model (FPFLM) and Karen Clark & Company (KCC) came from an incorrect table. Their comparison of population-wide model estimates with claims made to the US National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) also inadequately accounted for low insurance take-up and resulting risk-selection bias. They said these issues may affect core conclusions and would undertake a comprehensive revision.

Limits and context

The notice gives no corrected replacement estimates. It does not establish that any named model is accurate. This case does not claim that NFIP officials used this paper or that it changed insurance premiums. PreventionWeb says its content is posted by community members or editors and is not necessarily UNDRR’s view. Platform distribution is not UN endorsement. The notice says all three authors agree to retract for comprehensive revision. The 4% claim is in the live summary. The original paper’s abstract also gives roughly twice-as-high First Street losses and approximately 13-times-lower KCC losses. Those magnitudes should not be attributed to the summary itself. The exact page publication date is unavailable; 24 February is its displayed last-checked date.

How the claim connects to the study

PreventionWeb names the exact three authors and research title. Its Access link directly opens the publisher PDF at journalofcrr.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/JCRR-Dusseau-et-al-Volume-04-Article-01-February-2026.pdf, whose first page identifies DOI 10.63024/k6yh-a2v6. The publisher’s 16 September retraction explicitly targets that DOI. The retrieved PreventionWeb summary has no retraction warning and continues to describe the findings as model validation.
