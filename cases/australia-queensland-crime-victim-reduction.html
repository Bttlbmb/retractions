Government safety claims still exaggerate a corrected fall in crime victims

Queensland Government · Premier and Youth Justice Minister

Why it matters

The exaggerated reduction is 4.3 percentage points, or approximately 66%, above the corrected one. It remains in the government’s own account of the success of controversial youth-sentencing and policing policies. That account includes explicit criticism of the previous Labor government. This affects how readers assess the claimed results.

Still says

7 October 2025
Victim numbers down 10.8% per capita this year

Headline bullet; repeated in opening and policy-results paragraphs · Open the source ↗

A joint government release credits stronger laws and police with a 10.8% per-capita fall in crime victims. It compares January–September 2025 with the same period of 2024. It presents this alongside its Adult Crime, Adult Time youth-sentencing policy.

What changed

Corrected15 October 2025

Police Minister Dan Purdie told parliament that human error produced the wrong 10.8% reduction. It arose in manual compilation and analysis of the January–September victim rate. The actual reduction per capita was 6.5%. He apologised and said updated figures had been requested for the website.

Limits and context

The corrected data still show a reduction; this is not a reversal into rising crime. Neither the original comparison nor the correction establishes that Adult Crime, Adult Time caused the fall. The separate 2,986 youths charged and category-specific victim reductions are not treated as corrected. That is because the retrieved ministerial correction specifically identifies the overall per-capita victim reduction. No deliberate deception or permanent suppression of the correction is claimed; the minister publicly corrected the record in parliament. The issue is that government pages still carry the old figure without a correction.

How the claim connects to the study

The live 7 October joint release expressly attributes its January–September 2025 victim numbers to Queensland Police data. The 15 October ministerial statement explicitly identifies the 7 October media event, the same months, the old 10.8% rate and the corrected 6.5% rate. Both the ministerial-release page and the Premier's separate mirror still repeat the old figure without a visible correction.
