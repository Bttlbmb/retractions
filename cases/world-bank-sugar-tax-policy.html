A World Bank tax-policy factsheet retains the original sugar-levy benefit estimate

World Bank · international development bank · Kazakhstan sugar-tax Q&A

Why it matters

The retained reduction is about 3.7 times the corrected estimate. This changes the magnitude of evidence presented for a politically contested tax policy; it does not show that the levy had no benefit.

Still says

23 November 2021 · current public factsheet
the sugar content in these drinks was about 30g lower per household per week, a 10% decrease.

Question 1 · Are taxes on sugary drinks really effective in lowering consumption of sugar? · Open the source ↗

The World Bank’s Q&A supporting consideration of a sugary-drink tax in Kazakhstan says the UK levy reduced sugar purchases by 30 g per household per week, or 10%, while the drink volume stayed unchanged.

What changed

Replaced5 Dec 2023

The BMJ retracted the paper after an incorrectly calculated, redundant weighting variable altered its policy-relevant results. The corrected study estimates 8 g (2.7%) less sugar purchased per household per week, not 30 g (about 10%). It estimates 189 mL (2.6%) more soft drinks purchased, rather than no change.

Limits and context

The analysis concerns drinks bought to take home and an estimated counterfactual from preannouncement trends. It is not about all sugar consumption or directly measured health effects. The corrected study still finds lower sugar purchases and no increase in confectionery or alcohol purchases. These pages predate the retraction, which is from 2023. This is an older correction with continuing public availability.

How the claim connects to the study

The factsheet’s The research found hyperlink is DOI 10.1136/bmj.n254. It gives the same 31 million-purchase panel and affected 30 g/10% result, then draws a tax-policy lesson for Kazakhstan. The formal notice identifies that exact DOI and corrected replacement.
