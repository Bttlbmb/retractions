AOK’s parent guide retains the original decline in youth gaming disorder

AOK · German public health insurer group

Why it matters

The article uses the estimate to introduce advice to parents about in-game purchases, warning signs and support for children.

Still says

24 September 2024
Von den 10- bis 17-Jährigen, die mindestens einmal pro Woche digitale Spiele nutzen, zeigen 4,3 Prozent ein krankhaftes Spielverhalten

Frequency of gaming disorder among children · Open the source ↗

In EnglishAOK’s medically reviewed parent article describes pathological gaming as affecting 4.3% of 10–17-year-olds who play at least weekly, down from 6.3% in 2022.

What changed

Corrected8 Sept 2026

DAK-Gesundheit (DAK) and University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf corrected how the survey applied its thresholds. The 2023 estimate rose to 5.9%. The corrected report describes the 2022 and 2023 rates as statistically similar.

Limits and context

AOK describes the denominator as children playing at least weekly. The corrected report’s prevalence result is for the surveyed 10–17-year-olds generally. The 5.9% should therefore be described with the study’s denominator.

How the claim connects to the study

The article names the German Centre for Addiction Research in Childhood and Adolescence, identifies the summer 2023 wave and repeats its original 4.3% and 6.3% comparison. The corrected report supplies the revised estimate and test.
