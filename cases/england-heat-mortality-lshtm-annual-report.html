MajorUnited Kingdom·Climate & environment

LSHTM’s annual report retains the original heat-mortality figure as a research case study

London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine·Annual accounts for the year ended 31 July 2024

Corrected24 Jul 2026

Still says

51,670 deaths in England, and 2,186 deaths in Wales, were associated with the hottest days over the 35 years from 1988 to 2022.

Printed page 4 · climate and mortality case study · Open the source ↗

LSHTM’s annual accounts cite 51,670 deaths associated with England’s hottest days from 1988 to 2022 in a case study of the institution’s climate and health research.

What changed

The Office for National Statistics identified 51,670 as an incorrect estimate in July 2026. Its methods guide says an error in the analysis code substantially overestimated deaths associated with the hottest days at England level.

The ONS notice names the England total of 51,670. The August 2026 replacement reports 40,063 for a longer period, 1988–2025, with updated methods and a narrower definition of the hottest days.

Why it matters

The annual report uses the figure to explain a researcher’s contribution to the ONS analysis and the need for climate and public-health measures.

How the claim connects to the study

The case study names the ONS report and LSHTM contributor Antonio Gasparrini, and reproduces the exact original total singled out in the later ONS correction.
