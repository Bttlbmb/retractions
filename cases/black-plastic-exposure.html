International·Health

A shopping guide reports one correction but retains a later-corrected exposure estimate

Shop TODAY·Updated September 2025

Corrected3 Jul 2025

Still says

According to the study, an exposure of about 34,700 nanograms per day was present from utensils tested

Are black cooking utensils toxic? · Open the source ↗

The utensil replacement guide still gives an estimated BDE-209 exposure of 34,700 nanograms per day.

What changed

A second correction reduced that estimate to 7,900 ng/day after the authors identified a misinterpreted exposure formula.

The page does explain the first correction, which raised the reference dose from 42,000 to 420,000 ng/day. The missing update concerns the exposure estimate itself. Neither correction withdrew the measured detection of flame retardants.

Why it matters

The number appears in a consumer safety explanation alongside recommended replacement utensils and affiliate shopping links.

How the claim connects to the study

The guide links the original Chemosphere paper and its first corrigendum. The July 2025 corrigendum separately corrects the numerator that the guide continues to report.

Liu, Brandsma & Schreder · 2024 · Chemosphere
