A teacher-education article retains withdrawn evidence for classroom AI benefits

Medienimpulse · Austrian education-ministry journal·21 March 2026

Retracted22 Apr 2026

Still says

Eine Meta-Analyse über 51 experimentelle Studien weist einen signifikant positiven Effekt von ChatGPT auf höheres Denken nach

Page 48 · section 6.3; duration argument on page 51 · Open the source ↗

A current professional education article presents the withdrawn meta-analysis as evidence that ChatGPT improves higher-order thinking, and uses its duration results to argue that teachers and learners need sufficient experience with AI.

What changed

The journal editor retracted the meta-analysis because discrepancies undermined confidence in its analysis and conclusions.

This article was published before the retraction and remains available without a source-withdrawal notice. Its own survey of teachers in Baden-Württemberg and other cited studies are separate. The article is professional educational commentary and research, not an official ministry policy. This is another use of the same Wang–Fan paper already documented in the German parliamentary case; the withdrawal does not establish that all classroom AI benefits or the article’s recommendations are false.

Why it matters

The Austrian education ministry publishes the journal and links it from its current school-practice resources. The article uses the affected evidence in a discussion of classroom access to AI and teacher development.

How the claim connects to the study

The article repeats the meta-analysis’s higher-order-thinking effect (g=0.457) on pages 3 and 48, then uses its duration results on page 51. Page 58 gives the exact Wang–Fan title and original DOI. The current article page and issue contents offer the same PDF without a source-retraction update.
