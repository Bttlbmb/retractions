MajorInternational·Public health

A vaccine advocacy article retains the SIDS inference rejected when its source was removed

Children’s Health Defense · US non-profit advocacy organisation · German-language Defender article

Why it matters

The article uses the study in public vaccine-safety advocacy. The same paper’s removal became a political oversight issue in 2026: a US Senate investigation chair requested the journal’s records and cited a separate inquiry by the US health secretary.

Still says

3 August 2021 · German-language article remains publicly available
die zeitliche Häufung von SIDS-Todesfällen in Verbindung mit Impfungen

Paragraphs on temporal clustering, uniform distribution and the author’s main findings · Open the source ↗

In EnglishThe German-language article reports on 2,605 infant deaths reported to the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS). It says 58% occurred within three days after vaccination and 78% within seven days. It presents this clustering, including a subset of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS) reports, as evidence of a vaccine–infant-death connection. It says a uniform distribution would be expected without one.

What changed

Withdrawn9 Apr 2026

Toxicology Reports removed the study on 9 April 2026. Its formal notice identifies serious methodological flaws in inferring a vaccination–SIDS correlation from VAERS data. It says temporal clustering is expected in passive reports regardless of causality, so the methods did not support the conclusions.

Limits and context

The publisher’s exact action is REMOVED; this catalogue groups it under Withdrawn. The author disputes the removal. The notice rejects the correlation inference but gives no replacement values for the reported 58% and 78%. These percentages describe selected VAERS infant-death reports, not all vaccinated infants or their probability of death. The 2,605 reports cover all infant deaths. The SIDS subset has 1,048 reports. The advocacy article predates the removal and has no update about it in its retrieved text. Neither political inquiry establishes the study’s validity or a resulting policy change.

How the claim connects to the study

The article names Neil Z. Miller, Toxicology Reports and the 1990–2019 VAERS analysis. Its opening study hyperlink leads to ScienceDirect identifier S2214750021001268, the original paper linked to DOI 10.1016/j.toxrep.2021.06.020 in PubMed. The formal removal notice names that exact title and publication and addresses the inference retained in the article.
