MajorInternational·Health

An OECD policy report retains a withdrawn English autism prevalence finding

OECD

Why it matters

The international policy report uses the English survey to explain how rising diagnoses relate to underlying prevalence, informing discussion of autism assessment and support.

Still says

26 March 2026
Autism prevalence has remained relatively stable over time, at around 1%

Chapter 2 · Box 2.2, Figure 2.2 and Table 2.2 · Open the source ↗

The OECD report presents adult autism prevalence in England as broadly stable over time and gives the withdrawn 2023/24 estimate of 900 per 100,000 adults, equivalent to 0.9%.

What changed

Withdrawn18 May 2026

NHS England withdrew the 2023/24 autism findings in May 2026. Its October replacement explains a case-classification error and unexplained variation between interviewers: no reliable prevalence estimate or comparison with 2014 can be derived.

The affected statistic describes adults in England. The OECD report discusses autism policy internationally and was published before the withdrawal. The 2007 and 2014 survey estimates remain valid. The withdrawn 2023/24 findings cannot establish whether prevalence changed since 2014.

How the claim connects to the study

Box 2.2 and Figure 2.2 compare APMS 2007, 2014 and 2023/24. Table 2.2 gives England’s latest estimate. Reference 59 links the exact NHS England autism chapter that was subsequently withdrawn.
