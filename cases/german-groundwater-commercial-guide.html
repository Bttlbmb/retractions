Germany·Climate & environment

A water-system seller still distributes the original version of a corrected groundwater report

GreenLife GmbH·Undated product-advice article · still distributed

CorrectedJuly 2025

Still says

Schon jetzt wird mehr entnommen, als sich nachbildet.

BUND foreword, signed Verena Graichen · embedded report page 4 · Open the source ↗

The article defines groundwater stress as extraction exceeding natural replenishment and embeds the report’s original BUND foreword claiming that extraction already exceeds replenishment.

What changed

BUND corrected the over-extraction claim. The study’s stress classification does not mean extraction exceeds all replenishment; its revised foreword removes that assertion.

The study retains its 201-district stress classification. Structural stress uses a threshold of 20% of recharge; acute stress concerns falling levels. The corrected interpretation does not establish that water shortages are absent or assess the seller’s products.

Why it matters

The article promotes rainwater capture and greywater recycling and directly links a rainwater-system product. Its embedded study copy remains the old edition even though its separate source link now opens the corrected original.

How the claim connects to the study

The current GreenLife article embeds and offers its own old 36-page PDF. A fresh file comparison shows its BUND foreword, signed by Verena Graichen, retains the removed sentence and lacks the correction note. BUND’s current PDF explicitly records substantive corrections to the foreword and chapter 6b. Primary BUND and ISOE statements explain the overuse-versus-stress correction.

Robert Lütkemeier & David Kuhn · 2025 · ISOE · commissioned by BUND
Grundwasserstress in Deutschland: Struktureller und akuter Grundwasserstress durch öffentliche und nichtöffentliche Entnahmen auf Ebene der Landkreise
