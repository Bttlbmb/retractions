An energy-policy release still presents conclusions from a temporarily withdrawn UHERO report

Mirage News · Australian news site republishing press releases · syndicated University of Hawaiʻi release

Why it matters

The release presents a preferred long-term electricity strategy and billion-dollar savings, though the report was withdrawn for errors. These conclusions concern decisions about power generation, LNG infrastructure, ratepayer costs and island-grid reliability. In a later Hawaiʻi Public Radio (HPR) interview, the author acknowledged an AI-generated inversion of a solar-price trend. The author acknowledged errors that overstated the case against the LNG project.

Still says

30 June 2026 · currently available syndication
The report also found that Oʻahu does not need another large fuel-burning power plant beyond projects already under development.

Body · report findings, immediately after the $3.4 billion estimate · Open the source ↗

A syndicated university release says Oʻahu needs no new large fuel-burning power plant beyond those already under development. It says solar-procurement reform could save customers about $3.4 billion during 2027–2050. It presents avoiding the JERA liquefied natural gas (LNG) bundle as part of the cheapest electricity pathway.

What changed

Withdrawn7 July 2026 · temporary withdrawal

On 7 July 2026, the University of Hawaiʻi announced that the faculty author was temporarily withdrawing the report to correct identified errors, complete a comprehensive review, obtain stakeholder feedback and republish a revised edition.

Limits and context

This is a temporary author withdrawal of the June report. It is not a journal retraction or proof that solar is more expensive than LNG. The university notice does not itemize the errors. The AI and cost details are attributed to the author through HPR’s interview and reporting. No independently verified replacement numerical estimate for the $3.4 billion saving was obtained. The University of Hawaiʻi Economic Research Organization’s 31 August 2026 seminar page discusses a same-title analysis under a preliminary-research warning. It still favours solar and storage over new fuel plants. It cannot be used to claim that the policy conclusion was reversed or that research ceased. The State Energy Office critique is a separate, interested government assessment, not the publisher’s correction. Mirage calls this a point-in-time public release, but the retrieved article has no withdrawal notice.

How the claim connects to the study

Mirage explicitly syndicates the University of Hawaiʻi release and links the exact the University of Hawaiʻi Economic Research Organization (UHERO) report slug three-findings-on-hawaiʻis-electricity-future. Its original UH news and report URLs now return 404. The university's 7 July notice names the same full report title and records the temporary withdrawal for errors. The stale release predates that withdrawal and retains the findings without a withdrawal warning.
