A pharmacy publication retains workplace advice from an AI study withdrawn in August 2026

Pharmazeutische Zeitung · German weekly newspaper for pharmacists

Why it matters

The German pharmacy publication turns the study into practical advice for working and studying with AI. Its original publisher has already marked the source release as retracted.

Still says

Article published 27 April 2026 · still available
Mindestens zwei bis drei »KI-freie« Tage pro Woche

Practical tips · Auf die Art der Nutzung kommt es an · Open the source ↗

In EnglishThe article retains the finding that 58% reported AI did most of the thinking and reproduces advice to plan two or three AI-free days each week.

What changed

Retracted31 Aug 2026

The journal withdrew the study after statistical and methodological inconsistencies and unavailable participant data prevented verification of the reported findings.

Limits and context

The article explicitly says the observational design cannot prove causation. The advice is attributed to the researcher and was not tested as an intervention. The retraction removes this study as verified support; it does not demonstrate that the advice itself is harmful or ineffective.

How the claim connects to the study

The full article directly links the original APA paper and DOI, names Sarah Baldeo and reports the 1,923-participant sample. It also quotes the APA release, which now prominently reports the 31 August retraction. The German article retains the findings and advice without that update.
