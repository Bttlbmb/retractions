The 2025 AI safety report still uses a withdrawn study as evidence of scientific productivity

International AI Safety Report · UK parliamentary deposit

Why it matters

The report was produced to inform AI policymaking. Its 2025 web edition remains available alongside a newer edition, and the original PDF is supplied through the UK Parliament’s deposited-papers service.

Still says

29 January 2025 · older edition still offered
recently observed large productivity impacts of narrow AI systems on scientific discovery

Section 2.3.1 · printed/PDF page 117; reference 713 on page 265 · Open the source ↗

The 2025 report treats large productivity gains from narrow AI in scientific discovery as observed evidence, then suggests such gains could extend to general-purpose AI. Its supporting reference is the withdrawn Toner-Rodgers preprint.

What changed

Withdrawn20 May 2025

arXiv administrators withdrew the preprint over concerns about data validity and incomplete ethics-review requirements. MIT had already stated that the findings should not be relied on in academic or public discussion.

This case concerns the older 2025 edition, published before withdrawal. A 2026 edition is now available and does not cite Toner-Rodgers. The 2025 passage is a tentative extrapolation, not a demonstrated general-purpose-AI effect or a policy recommendation. Its separate economic-growth reference (714) and other productivity studies are unaffected.

How the claim connects to the study

The affected scientific-discovery clause cites reference 713, which names Toner-Rodgers and the exact preprint title. Both the current 2025 web text and the parliamentary PDF retain the passage without a source-withdrawal note. The arXiv record identifies the same paper and its administrative withdrawal on 20 May 2025.

Aidan Toner-Rodgers · 2024 · arXiv preprint · not a published journal article
