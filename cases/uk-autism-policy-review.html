A UK government-commissioned autism review retains a withdrawn prevalence finding

Department of Health and Social Care · independent interim review·31 March 2026 · interim report · updated 10 June 2026

Withdrawn18 May 2026

Still says

the examined prevalence of autism in adults has remained broadly stable at around 1 in 100

Section 8 · Population prevalence · Open the source ↗

The UK government-commissioned interim review says adult autism prevalence remained broadly stable across 2007, 2014 and 2023/24. It includes the subsequently withdrawn 0.9% estimate for 2023/24.

What changed

NHS England withdrew the 2023/24 autism findings in May 2026. Its October replacement explains a case-classification error and unexplained variation between interviewers: no reliable prevalence estimate or comparison with 2014 can be derived.

The interim report acknowledges wide confidence intervals. The October final review changes its treatment of adult prevalence and does not repeat the withdrawn 0.9%. The interim text remains separately available. Earlier survey estimates are unaffected, and the withdrawal leaves change since 2014 unresolved.

Why it matters

The review examines rising diagnoses and demand for assessment and support to inform recommendations to the UK government.

How the claim connects to the study

Section 8 gives the APMS estimates for 2007, 2014 and 2023/24. Reference 2 identifies the NHS England survey. The latest estimate and comparison depend on the chapter that NHS England subsequently withdrew.
