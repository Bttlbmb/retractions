United Kingdom·Health

Policy reports retain a withdrawn estimate of adult autism prevalence

OECD · Department of Health and Social Care·OECD report 26 March 2026 · DHSC interim report 31 March 2026

Withdrawn18 May 2026

Still says

Autism prevalence has remained relatively stable over time, at around 1%

Chapter 2 · Box 2.2, Figure 2.2 and Table 2.2 · Open the source ↗

OECD presents autism prevalence in England as stable through 2023/24, including an estimate of 900 per 100,000 adults. A UK government-hosted interim review retains the same 0.9% estimate.

What changed

NHS England withdrew the 2023/24 autism findings in May 2026. Its October replacement explains a case-classification error and unexplained variation between interviewers: no reliable prevalence estimate or comparison with 2014 can be derived.

The interim report acknowledges wide confidence intervals. A final review published in October replaces its treatment of adult prevalence with earlier-survey estimates and does not repeat 0.9%. This case concerns the unchanged interim text still distributed separately. The 2007 and 2014 estimates remain valid; withdrawal does not establish that prevalence actually rose.

Why it matters

The findings frame debates about rising diagnoses, eligibility for support and service design in a current international policy report and an older UK interim review.

How the claim connects to the study

OECD’s Box 2.2 and Figure 2.2 compare APMS 2007, 2014 and 2023/24; Table 2.2 gives England’s latest estimate. Reference 59 links the exact NHS autism chapter. The DHSC interim review independently identifies the same survey in reference 2.
