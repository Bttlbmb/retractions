A benefits-reform opinion article retains JRF’s corrected child-poverty impact estimate

Big Issue · Iain Porter (JRF senior policy adviser)

Why it matters

The child-poverty impact used to advocate a benefits reform is eight times the corrected estimate; the total-person estimate is about 4.3 times the corrected value. JRF says its policy narrative and conclusions would not change, but the magnitude of the claimed immediate benefit changes substantially.

Still says

13 November 2025 · currently available article
lift 130,000 people (including 80,000 children) out of poverty immediately.

First proposed system change · protected minimum floor paragraph · Open the source ↗

A Big Issue opinion article by JRF’s Iain Porter recommends a protected minimum floor in Universal Credit at the coming Budget, saying it would lift 130,000 people, including 80,000 children, out of poverty immediately.

What changed

Corrected2026 · exact correction date unclear

JRF disclosed a coding error in its original October 2025 briefing. For the same 2025/26 scenario, the projected number lifted out of poverty should have been 30,000 people, including 10,000 children, rather than 130,000 and 80,000.

This compares the original and corrected coding for the same 2025/26 policy scenario. It must not be confused with JRF’s new 2027/28 estimates after the two-child limit was abolished, or its separate estimates of very deep poverty. The briefing defines poverty as income after housing costs below 60% of the median and very deep poverty as below 40%. JRF warns of relatively low sample sizes and says it is updating later modelling. The correction page has an 11 September 2026 publication date and a further update after 22 September, but no separate timestamp for the coding correction; its exact date remains unclear.

How the claim connects to the study

The article is authored by Iain Porter, one of the JRF briefing authors, and repeats its original 130,000/80,000 pair for the protected-minimum-floor policy. Its Mimecast-wrapped policy hyperlink was followed to JRF’s corrected replacement briefing. Note 6 explicitly identifies and corrects the original pair for 2025/26. The article predates the correction.
